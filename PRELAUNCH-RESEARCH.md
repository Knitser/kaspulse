# kaspulse — pre-launch improvement roadmap

*Extended research, 2026-07-24. Method: 7 discovery lenses (oracle SOTA, exploit post-mortems, long-tail pricing, Kaspa ecosystem, competitive, code audit, launch ops) → 49 raw recommendations → dedup/rank → 12 adversarially verified against the live deployment and on-chain pools → this roadmap. 21 agents. Agents measured the LIVE oracle and real pool reserves, not just the docs.*

## Headline

The cryptography, the aggregation guards and the product surface are all better than almost anything shipped by one person on a young chain — but three things in the live artifact are currently false or exploitable, and all three are findable with a single curl. The worst is that `covenant.signatures` is 5 committee sigs over `blake2b(price_bytes(price_e8))` — a bare integer with no pair, exponent, round or timestamp (src/main.rs:413-419, :514) — so BTC/USD's published sigs unlock any `price_gate_redeem` covenant on any pair with a lower strike, and BONKEY/KANDA/KOMA publish price_e8=0 with valid sigs over blake2b(empty), permanently satisfying every AtOrBelow gate. The single most valuable change before launch is to delete that field and the doc lines recommending it (an hour), then re-derive it bound to pair+expo+round. Everything else pre-launch is honesty repair: `"twap":true` is a hardcoded literal on all 61 feeds, `round` resets to 1 on every restart and manufactures real equivocation proofs against your own committee, `thin` certifies pools where $1.44 buys a 10% move, `freshest_ms` is the age of the RPC read on pools whose reserves last moved 16 days ago (median across all 65), and the site claims seven exchanges while Bybit contributes zero of 61 feeds and OKX's KAS-USDT does not exist as an instrument. None of this is hard; it is roughly three focused days.

## Biggest risk

Someone reads /v1/feed on announcement day and posts a three-line proof that your flagship on-chain primitive is forgeable. It takes one curl: grab BTC/USD's `covenant.price_bytes` and its five valid committee signatures, and they unlock any hosted-committee price_gate_redeem whose strike is lower — plus BONKEY/KANDA/KOMA publish price_e8=0 with real signatures over blake2b(empty), so every AtOrBelow gate is permanently satisfiable. It hurts far more than a normal bug for three reasons: docs/MESSAGE-FORMAT.md §8 and web/guide.html:184 explicitly tell readers to use those signatures in production; src/consumer_live.rs:52-55 — the TN10 demo you cite as proof — builds its committee from the real kaspulse-node keys, so the vulnerable path is the shipped, publicly-linked artifact, not a hypothetical; and it lands directly on the "verify it yourself, nothing is hypothetical" brand, where a competent stranger verifying it and finding it broken is the exact failure mode you have no story for. Two independent reviewers found it from opposite directions (live JSON vs. reading the redeem script) without collaborating — that is your ETA after the announcement. Mitigation is one hour: delete the `cov_sigs_json` line at src/main.rs:514 and the `"signatures"` field at :555, make sdk verify_covenant() hard-error, and fix the four doc locations — two of which currently claim the opposite of each other. Then do the bound preimage properly and re-run the TN10 proof before you claim it again. Runner-up, same shape: the round counter resetting to 1 on every restart, which turns your equivocation-slashing headline into thousands of valid slashing proofs against yourself, harvestable for free by anyone who polled across a deploy.

### 1. Withdraw the unbound covenant signature (do this first, today)
**Effort:** 1 hour for Tier A; ~1 day for Tier B (fold into the v3 bump)

**Why:** Anyone can curl /v1/feed/BTC-USD, copy `covenant.price_bytes` (6.5e12) plus its five valid committee sigs, and spend ANY hosted-committee price gate whose strike is lower — which is every realistic KAS or KRC-20 gate. It lands worse than a normal bug because docs/MESSAGE-FORMAT.md §8 and web/guide.html:182-184 actively tell readers to use these sigs in production, and src/consumer_live.rs:52-55 — the TN10 proof you cite publicly — builds its committee from the REAL kaspulse-node-{0,1,2}.key files, not a demo set. Two independent reviewers found this from opposite directions; that is roughly how long it survives after the announcement.

**Change:** TIER A, ~1 hour, ship before anything else: delete the `cov_sigs_json` line at src/main.rs:514; drop `"signatures":[{cov_sigs_json}]` from `cov_j` at src/main.rs:555; change /v1/committee's `"covenant":"blake2b(price_bytes)"` at src/main.rs:468; make sdk/src/lib.rs:235-250 `verify_covenant()` hard-error with "unbound covenant domain withdrawn". TIER B, ~1 day, ship with the v3 bump: new single-blob preimage `"kaspulse/cov/v2" ‖ blake2b256(PAIR)[0..8] ‖ expo(i8) ‖ round_be(u64) ‖ ts_be(u64) ‖ mant_le`, signed as blake2b256 of that, emitted as `covenant.preimage` + `covenant.signatures`. Sign mant+expo, NOT price_e8 — six live pairs lose >1% to e8 quantization (yoni 27.9%, GTEST 3.9%, and BONKEY/KANDA/KOMA quantize to literally zero). Rebuild the script in sdk/src/lib.rs:363-397 with OpSubstr (0x7f, verified enabled at the pinned rusty-kaspa rev): `OpDup <0> <24> OpSubstr <baked_24B_prefix> OpEqualVerify` binds tag+pair+expo, then value-compare the mant tail, then the existing OpBlake2b + committee_tail. ### 2. Make `round` a monotonic wall-clock slot and rotate the five committee keys
**Effort:** 1 hour for the slot + rotation; +2 hours for the 32-byte record

**Why:** src/main.rs:692 is `let mut round = 1u64;` with zero persistence, and src/main.rs:421 defines the bond record as blake2b(pair)[0..8] ‖ round_be ‖ mant_be. Live /health returns round 816430 at 334,734s uptime — the counter IS the uptime. Every restart replays slots 1..N at different prices under the same persistent keys, so anyone who archived /v1/feed across one deploy holds thousands of genuine, script-verifiable equivocation proofs against the committee that built the equivocation-slashing covenant. Nobody loses money today (the bond is TN10-only), but it silently voids your headline on-chain claim, and the fix is an hour. Key rotation is free right now and impossible after launch: nobody has TOFU-pinned /v1/committee yet, so rotating orphans every already-published pre-fix signature. After the announcement, rotation is a breaking event for every integrator.

**Change:** src/main.rs:692-697: delete the counter; hoist `let mut hwm: u64 = fs::read_to_string("round.hwm").ok().and_then(|s| s.trim().parse().ok()).unwrap_or(0);` and inside the loop use `let slot = now_ms() / SERVE_MS; let round = slot.max(hwm + 1); hwm = round;`, persisting every 150 rounds (~60s). Use MS resolution — `now_s()/TICK_S` would put 2-3 different mantissas in one slot at SERVE_MS=400 and manufacture equivocation continuously, strictly worse than today. Log a `KASPULSE_ROUND_REGRESSION` token if slot <= hwm. Same bug and same fix at src/signer.rs:63. Then run scripts/setup-keys.sh, mint five fresh keys, set KASPULSE_NODE_KEYS + KASPULSE_REQUIRE_KEYS=1, and redeploy AFTER the slot fix lands. Same commit if you can: widen attestation_record to 32 bytes with `expo` appended (src/main.rs:421 AND the duplicate at sdk/src/lib.rs:432 — they will silently diverge otherwise), shift the OpSubstr indices in bond_redeem and bond_redeem_with_reclaim from (16,24) to (16,32), and change the pair-id derivation to blake2b("kaspulse/bond/v2|{pair}") so a v1 record can never share a slot with a v2 one. Without expo, mant=293800000 @ expo=-10 and @ expo=-9 produce byte-identical records — a 10x price move is provably unslashable. Re-run `cargo run --bin slash --features onchain` (the 4-case script-VM proof).

### Start persisting price history on launch day — it cannot be backfilled

**Why:** Nothing is persisted anywhere. The in-memory `hist` ring (HISTORY entries) dies on every restart, so there is no auditable price record and no falsifiable uptime record. ## Lens key findings (the measurements behind the above)

### Oracle design state of the art — aggregation, uncertainty publication, TWAP construction, and long-tail/single-venue handling, benchmarked against Pyth, Chainlink (Data Feeds + DEX State Price Streams), RedStone, and Uniswap V2/V3 oracle primitives, then mapped onto kaspulse's actual live behaviour and pool set.

I measured kaspulse's real pool set rather than trusting the flags, and the headline is that the KRC-20 feeds are far less "live" than the API claims. Across all 65 discovered pools, the median time since the last on-chain trade (blockTimestampLast from getReserves) was 16.4 days; 44 of 65 had not traded in over 24 hours; and not a single pool — including the deepest, NACHO at ~957k WKAS — had traded within 28 minutes of sampling. Yet every KRC-20 feed publishes freshest_ms around 6,700ms and no staleness signal, because STALE_MS=30s measures how recently kaspulse polled the RPC, not how recently the price moved; NACHO/USD currently reports freshest_ms 6732 on a pool whose reserves last changed ~2 hours ago, and puppy/USD is a 304-day-old constant multiplied by a live KAS/USD. Depth is the second problem: MIN_LIQ_WKAS=1000 is ~$29.50 at today's KAS, so the binary thin flag fires on only 22 of 65 pools, while my constant-product math shows 62 of 65 pools can have their published price moved +10% for under $1,000 and 53 of 65 for under $100 — KDOG, MARK, LUL, FDBR, KBROS and NOISE all pass as NOT thin at roughly $25-$30 to move 10%. Structurally, 51 of the 58 KRC-20 symbols have exactly one pool, so "median of independent venues" is N=1 and the API reports spread_bps: 0.0 and low == high == price, which a consumer gating on spread will read as perfect agreement rather than as no cross-check at all. Two concrete code defects compound this: kas_usd() at src/main.rs:202-214 computes its own raw median of fresh CEX sources with no MAD filter and no circuit breaker, so the guards protect the published KAS/USD feed but not the KAS/USD denominator that multiplies all 58 KRC-20 prices; and "twap":true is a hardcoded literal at src/main.rs:560 emitted on all 61 feeds even though only KRC-20 pools pass through the TWAP window — BTC/USD and KAS/USD are instantaneous medians. On the positive side, I verified the DEX pipeline is arithmetically sound: pricing WETH, cbBTC, USDC and USDT through the same reserve path and comparing against kaspulse's own CEX medians gives -0.70%, -1.10%, -0.68% and -0.57% deviation, which means a cross-rate triangulation invariant on those four known-truth assets is both cheap and immediately usable, and price0CumulativeLast/price1CumulativeLast return live non-zero values on the Igra pools.

### LENS 2 — How oracles actually get exploited: mapping real manipulation incidents onto kaspulse's exact parameters and code paths

The headline finding is not price manipulation at all — it is that kaspulse's on-chain attestation is unbound. `price_bytes()` (src/main.rs:413) encodes ONLY the integer price_e8: no pair, no exponent, no timestamp, no round. The redeem script (sdk/src/lib.rs:363-397) is `OpDup <strike_e8> OpGreaterThanOrEqual OpVerify <committee CSFS>`, so it verifies committee sigs over a bare integer. I pulled live sigs from /v1/feed: BTC/USD publishes price_bytes `407d62ffee05` (6,524,045,000,000) with 5 valid committee signatures. Those signatures unlock ANY price_gate_redeem covenant on ANY pair whose strike_e8 is below that number — a "pay if NACHO/USD >= $0.00002" gate (strike_e8=2000) is drainable today, for zero cost, with no manipulation, using the BTC attestation. `range_settle_redeem` is worse: with 61 feeds spanning 1e-10 to 6.5e4 and 120 rounds of history published per feed, essentially every range is always satisfiable. Absence of a timestamp also makes every attestation replayable forever — the Ostium class ($18M, July 2026, future-dated oracle timestamps). Second: the MAD filter is dead everywhere it matters. It requires >=4 sources (src/main.rs:445); the live catalog shows 51 of 58 KRC-20 feeds have exactly 1 source and 7 have 2 — none reach 4. Only the 3 majors are filtered, and KAS/USD flickers across the threshold (I observed n=3, Kraken absent, then n=4). Meanwhile web/index.html:76 tells the public every price is "a median of independent venues." Third: MIN_LIQ_WKAS=1000 WKAS is $29.50 at KAS $0.0295, so "not thin" certifies pools with $30 of depth — FLUX (1016 WKAS) passes; wstETH, a $2,410 asset, is priced off 937 WKAS ($27.65). Cost to double a thin pool runs $0.71 (GTEST) to $11.45 (wstETH), and to double a NOT-thin one, $12.41 (FLUX) to $75 (KASMO); the 60s window is a median of 12 samples, so an attacker needs to hold ~30s (6 samples), then eat the breaker's 12 rounds x 400ms = 4.8s. That is the entire defense. Fourth: `round` is a per-process counter starting at 1 (src/main.rs:692), so a restart replays slots — attestation_record is pairId||round||mant (src/main.rs:421), meaning every restart manufactures two validly-signed records with the same slot and different mant, which is exactly the slashing condition. An innocent nssm restart lets anyone slash the committee's own bond, and the resetting `round` in the envelope is a public beacon that the guards are down. It also binds mant but NOT expo, so signing 1.23e-9 vs 1.23e+3 produces identical record bytes — a 12-order-of-magnitude equivocation that is provably unslashable. Fifth: on restart both `bstate` and the TWAP `win` are empty, so the first round accepts any price (breaker bypass) and the first sample publishes spot as `median(w)` — while `"twap":true` is a hardcoded string literal (src/main.rs:560) that never reflects window fill. Sixth: `kas_usd()` (src/main.rs:201) multiplies all 58 KRC-20 prices by a raw staleness-only median that bypasses both the MAD filter and the circuit breaker protecting the published KAS/USD feed. On the lens's specific questions: (c) the breaker is NOT a meaningful DoS vector because it auto-releases after 4.8s — but that is precisely why it is not a real guard either, and because `lg` re-anchors to raw_med every non-breaking round it offers zero resistance to a staircase of sub-20% steps; (d) the peg check only attaches to Igra-sourced feeds (is_igra_source, src/main.rs:434), leaving 39 of 65 pools on Kasplex with no peg sanity check at all, and USDC already reads 0.9936 against a 2% tolerance — only 1.4% of headroom before it flags.

### Lens 3 — Pricing the long tail honestly: how kaspulse's 58 KRC-20 feeds should be aggregated, qualified, and described so that being *first* to price Kaspa-native assets becomes being *best*.

I measured the live deployment rather than trusting the design docs, and the long tail is in worse shape than the `thin` flag admits. **51 of 58 KRC-20 feeds are single-source** — "median of independent venues" is literally one `eth_call` — and every one of them publishes `spread_bps: 0.00`, which reads as perfect agreement when it actually means no second opinion exists. Of the 7 multi-venue feeds, the equal-weight `median()` at `src/main.rs:342` produces prices that are provably wrong or cheaply movable: **KREX/USD** publishes the midpoint of two venues that are **45% apart** (Kasplex 1.9317e-4 KAS vs Igra 2.8117e-4 KAS) — a price at which neither venue trades — and is `thin: false` because the `thin` test at `main.rs:545-547` folds **max** over per-chain liquidity while the *price* is an equal-weight blend including the shallowest pool. That inconsistency is exploitable: **IGRA/USD** blends a 2,177,603-WKAS Zealous pool with a 7,413-WKAS KaspaCom pool at 50/50 (a **294:1** depth ratio), so ~74 WKAS (≈$2.20) moves the shallow venue 2% and the published median ~1%; ~1,295 WKAS (≈$38, of which only round-trip slippage is actually lost) moves the published price ~19%, just under `BREAK_PCT`. Worse for the honesty story: I counted UniV2 `Swap` events over ~224,000 Kasplex blocks (~24h) and **31 of 39 pools had zero trades**; KREX's Kasplex pool has had zero swaps in 500,000 blocks (~2.2 days) while the API reports `freshest_ms` in the seconds. These are not prices of a market — they are the last state of an untouched pool, and Chainlink's own long-tail product ([State Pricing](https://chain.link/blog/state-pricing)) is explicit that it measures pool *state*, not execution. Finally, every serious provider ships a scalar uncertainty that kaspulse does not: Pyth publishes `conf` (distance from aggregate to the 25th/75th percentile of publisher votes) and DefiLlama returns a `confidence` field on literally every price (verified: `coins.llama.fi` returns `"confidence":0.99` for KAS). The good news is that the fixes are cheap — `pool_read()` at `main.rs:194-201` already computes both reserves and throws the token reserve away, and depth-at-slippage for a constant-product pool is a closed form (`dx = R_wkas·(√(1+δ)−1)`), so the entire depth/confidence layer is arithmetic on data he already fetches.

### LENS 6 — Codebase audit vs best practice (src/main.rs, src/http.rs, sdk/src/lib.rs, src/gate.rs, src/verify.rs, src/signer.rs, src/standing.rs), cross-checked against the live production feed at https://pulse.kascov.io

(1) The covenant-domain signatures are over `blake2b(price_bytes(price_e8))` with no pair, round or timestamp binding (main.rs:515, sdk/src/lib.rs:348-355) — so BTC/USD's live covenant sigs (price_e8 = 6,527,565,000,000) satisfy *any* `AtOrAbove` price gate for any pair, and BONKEY/KANDA/KOMA publish price_e8 = 0 with valid sigs over blake2b(empty), satisfying any `AtOrBelow` gate forever; both guide.html:184 and MESSAGE-FORMAT.md:183 explicitly tell people to use these sigs in production. (2) KAS/USD — the flagship feed of a Kaspa oracle — is currently served by exactly three REST, all-USDT-quoted venues at 2.2s freshness (BTC is at 47ms with six); Bybit is absent from all 61 feeds despite Bybit's KASUSDT endpoint responding fine, Kraken is absent from KAS despite KASUSD responding fine, and OKX's KAS-USDT (main.rs:135) does not exist as an instrument at all — nothing in the code or /metrics noticed across 3.86 days of uptime, and the MAD filter needs ≥4 sources so KAS/USD gets none. (3) `"twap":true` is a hardcoded literal (main.rs:560) that is emitted even when the window holds one sample, which is exactly the state after every restart, when `bstate` is also empty and the first observation becomes the circuit-breaker anchor unconditionally. Beyond those: BREAK_ROUNDS(12) × SERVE_MS(400ms) = 4.8s is shorter than SLOW_EVERY(5s), so the circuit breaker mathematically cannot hold anything that is sampled every 5 seconds — it provides zero protection for all 58 KRC-20 feeds and now for KAS/USD too — and during a hold it keeps re-signing the stale held price with a fresh `ts` and fresh covenant sigs every 5s. Finally, with exactly two venues the "median" is the arithmetic mean while `thin` uses `max` liquidity: KREX/USD's two pools disagree by 37% and MTGOX/USD by 26%, both published as the midpoint with thin=false, and USDC/USD — the sole input to the peg check gating 21 feeds — is itself a two-pool mean.

### Lens 7 — Launch readiness & adoption bar (ops/abuse + what a DeFi team actually requires)

Two real code bugs will fire on launch day, and neither is in the known-gaps list. (1) `round` is a bare in-process counter (`let mut round = 1u64;`, src/main.rs:692) with zero persistence — every service restart replays rounds 1..N with different prices, and since `attestation_record()` binds exactly (pair, round, mant), a restart manufactures cryptographically valid, publicly verifiable equivocation proofs against kaspulse's own committee keys. Equivocation-slashing is his headline on-chain feature; one nssm restart turns it into a loaded gun, and anyone who archived a feed response can post the two conflicting sigs. (2) The per-IP OG rate limiter keys on `s.peer_addr()` (src/http.rs `handle` → `og_rate_ok`), which behind Caddy is always 127.0.0.1 — so all 61 feeds' share cards share one global 30/min bucket, meaning a viral X post 429s its own preview images within seconds, and there is effectively no per-IP limiting anywhere on the service. Fixing (2) naively makes things worse, because `/og/*.png` re-renders the PNG on every single request with no memoization — it is the only CPU-bound endpoint and the real blast-radius vector to the other sites on the shared VPS. On the adoption side, the package gap is not decentralization (he documents that honestly); it is that nothing is persisted anywhere, so there is no auditable price history and no falsifiable uptime record — both Pyth (Benchmarks) and Chainlink (getRoundData / historical API) treat queryable history as table stakes, and history cannot be backfilled. Pyth itself went fully dark for 4+ hours in May 2026, which is the honest framing for a "what happens when kaspulse goes down" integrator page. Finally, orange-clouding pulse.kascov.io on Cloudflare's free tier requires moving the whole kascov.io zone's nameservers off Squarespace (CNAME/partial setup is Business-plan only) — which touches fees/rewind/boxes/402.kascov.io, so it is a days-ahead-of-launch operation, not a launch-morning one.
